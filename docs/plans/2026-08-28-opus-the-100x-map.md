# The 100x map — what was done, what is left, ranked by measured value

Status: IN-PROGRESS (2026-08-28, opus)

One session, 42 commits, three separate audits (a 106-agent workflow across
eight lenses, a boot-tax audit, and a gate-by-gate audit). This is the zoom-out:
the through-line, everything shipped, and the twenty approaches identified with
what each is actually worth — **including the ones that are worth nothing**,
because half the value of the audits was killing plausible ideas.

## The through-line: five ways to do less, four of them landed

Nothing here made anything *faster*. Every win came from not doing work:

| | do less by… | landed | worth |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | not re-running what could not have changed | **yes** | 309 s → 8.5 s on 70% of commits |
| 2 | not measuring what cannot breach a budget | **yes** | drawcheck 142 → 37 shots |
| 3 | not submitting frames nobody looks at | **yes** (earlier) | uxcheck 53 → 13 s |
| 4 | not building what is already built | **yes** | `warmup()`, batched corpus |
| 5 | not booting what can be reset | **partial** — 2 of 8 gates | 51.4 s available, ~13 s taken |

### The critical path today, and why the next win must remove work

After the `reachcheck` fix the suite is **71.7 s cold** and no single gate is the
pole any more. Per-gate, from the ledger:

    drawcheck    37.0     hydrocheck   11.7
    reachcheck   34.6     silrocks     11.7
    driftcheck   33.6     floatcheck   11.2
    integration  21.0     roadcheck    10.8
    uxcheck      18.8     heightcheck   8.6
    combatloop   17.3     creaturecheck 1.5   <- warm lease

**~184 s of browser work over 3 slots is a 61 s floor against 71.7 s actual.**
The three longest are balanced at ~35 s each, so the suite is now bound by
*total work ÷ slots* rather than by any one gate. That is why the 11 s taken off
`reachcheck` did not show up in the wall, and it is the single most important
thing to understand before optimising further: **shortening one gate now buys
about a third of itself.** The next real win has to remove work from several,
or remove a gate's work entirely.

`driftcheck` at 33.6 s is the least examined of the three and the obvious next
place to look — the audits priced its drive at a 3x 25,600-texel probe plus a
28-stop tour, and nobody has asked whether either needs to be that big.

### Why warm leases stop at two gates — the structural ceiling

`pageKey` is `build | WxH | query`, and **only `/shots` ever pools a page**, so
every page available to inherit is *shoot-mode at the capture viewport*. A gate
can only reuse one if it wants exactly that:

| gate | asks for | can inherit? |
|---|---|---|
| `heightcheck`, `creaturecheck` | shoot-mode, default viewport | **yes — 7.1x and 6.8x, landed** |
| `floatcheck` | shoot-mode, default viewport | yes, but **failed the verdict test** (91 vs 115 force-built) |
| `uxcheck` | `play: true`, so no `&shoot=1` in the query | **no** — measured 48.5 s → 46.8 s, which is noise, not a skipped boot |
| `integration`, `combatloop` | 1280x720 | **no** — viewport differs |
| `reachcheck` | shoot-mode | yes, but **failed** — its execution counts drift (93038 vs 93080), and this is the one gate whose whole purpose is "did this code run", so inheriting a page that already ran code is wrong in principle |
| `driftcheck` | — | **no** — its premise is "baseline, at a fresh boot" |

So the 51.4 s the workflow priced assumed every lease could go warm. **Three
could, two did.** Getting the rest needs one of two larger changes, and both
deserve their own measurement rather than an assumption:

1. **Standardise the play-gate viewports** to the capture one, so
   `integration` and `combatloop` can match. Cheap to try, but they are the two
   documented reuse burns, so each needs the byte-identical test.
2. **Pool play-mode pages.** Bigger, and the pages it would pool are exactly the
   driven ones that burned this repo — `releaseLease` throwing them away is the
   property that makes today's reuse safe. Do not undo it casually.

**A flag that can never fire is worse than no flag**, so `uxcheck` was tested,
passed the verdict check, and deliberately did NOT get `reuse`.

### Measured and killed — do not re-litigate

- **Scheduling and packing.** The suite is 100% browser-bound and near-perfectly
  packed: 233.5 browser slot-seconds ÷ 3 slots = 77.8 s ideal against 83.1 s
  actual. **5.3 s left, total.**
- **CPU-lane parallelism** (worker threads for silrocks, shared `Field` build,
  `tsc --incremental`). The CPU lane has **71 s of slack** and finishes at 11.9 s.
  Worth **0 s** until the browser lane drops below ~12 s.
- **More browser workers.** 4 browsers give 1.5x the throughput of one and
  **7.3x worse per-shot latency**. A gate suite is latency-bound.
- **Deleting the exclusive lease.** `cold` and the lock are different arguments
  to `pool.lease`. Saves zero boots; would let two tools drive one page.
- **Fixing "wasted" page boots.** 19 → 1 bought 16 s; the boot audit found
  **0 s of suite wall** in every variant. Leases boot 1.008 times each, forced.
- **`compileAsync` for shader warm-up** — 3% *slower*, already tried and reverted.
- **The ANGLE backend as the shader-cache culprit** — `gl` and `metal` both
  compile +181 programs on a *warm* load, time moving 3-6%. The cost is ANGLE's
  in-process GLSL→native translation, which no GPU disk cache can store.

Of 56 "big swings" the workflow proposed across eight lenses, **zero survived
adversarial verification**; 91 small wins did. That ratio is the finding.

## What the instruments cost and bought

Four new measuring tools, and every one of them changed a decision:

- **`resetcheck.mts`** — 35 state fields, one workload per gate known to burn a
  page, **a null arm that VOIDs if the digest moves on its own**. Found 29 leaks;
  is what made warm leases defensible rather than hopeful.
- **`drawwhere.mts`** — attributes every call through `renderBufferDirect`.
  Found 5.2 M triangles and 288 buckets behind a "496 calls, budget 800" that
  looked comfortable.
- **`warmquantum.mts`** — proved lazy bestiary construction moved counts, and
  proved `stableAfterWarmup`, which is what split "noise" into boot-vs-reuse.
- **`gatecache.inputsKey`** — the cache key, tested on 7 arms before being
  trusted.

## Six hypotheses died. That is the method, not a failure

`drawcheck` disagrees with itself on 25 of 142 shots by up to 60 calls. Killed,
in order: frame parity (`resetClock`), chunk sizing, wasted boots, state
accumulating across shots within a run, lazy bestiary construction, and the
enemy roster. Each was measured; each was wrong.

What survived is sharper than where it started: the gap is **perfectly
deterministic** — `579 514 514 514 514 574 514`, identical across three daemon
restarts — and tracks **boot-versus-reuse and nothing else**. A reused page
draws 60 fewer calls. The digest covers 35 fields and the answer is in the 36th.

**The general lesson is in `LANDMINES.md`:** nine unrelated shots landing on
exactly +15 is a discrete thing, not a distribution. Histogram the deltas before
modelling them as variance.
