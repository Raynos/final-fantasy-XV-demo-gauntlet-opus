## The order, and why it held

Seven plans were open. The ranking that governed the night:

1. **sibling-ports Wave 1** — the instruments, because they make everything
   after them falsifiable
2. **Phase 3** — boot and memory
3. **Phase 4** — content and gameplay
4. the two unowned perf failures
5. **procedural-modeling** — the art ceiling
6. **harness-daemon** — infra that pays back across sessions, not tonight
7. **process-lifts-from-kami** — three of five items already shipped

The instruments-first argument turned out to be the whole session's spine, and
in a way the plan did not predict: **four separate "known facts" were wrong**,
and every one of them was caught by an instrument rather than by reading.

## The wind

The night opened on the top item in the rescue ledger: the same shot came back
different alone versus sixth in a batch, 1.836 mean/255 against a 0.302 floor.
Every document on record said the cause was vegetation tile streaming.

It was not. Pinning vegetation streaming moved the number by **0.009**.

Streaming was genuinely nondeterministic — grass, scrub and trees each built
tiles against a `performance.now() + budgetMs` wall-clock deadline, so residency
depended on machine speed and on what the previous camera had cached — and that
is now fixed with a `converge()` called from `Game.settle`. But it was not what
anyone was seeing.

The carrier was the **wind**. `Weather.resetClock` set only `_snap`, a flag that
skips the lerp toward the target preset. The gust envelope `_gust` is integrated
forever and drives `windStrength` through three sines; `windDir` drifts
permanently; neither is part of `target`, so no preset change and no clock reset
had ever touched them. Probed directly: `windStrength` **0.840** on a page's
first shot, **0.944** after one other shot.

Grass, scrub, twigs and hair all sway off that number, which is why the
difference sat on thin silhouettes and read, to everyone who looked at the mean,
as noise. **1.836 → 0.340.** `imgdiff` at 1.5/255 means something now.

The lesson went into `LANDMINES.md` in the form that generalises: *when a visual
difference has no obvious carrier, print the state, do not stare at the frame.*

## Four lanes, then six

Agents ran in worktrees on disjoint directories, capped at four concurrent.
Boot, instruments, content, character art; then graphics-ceiling, atmosphere,
modeling, inventory as the first wave landed.

**Boot: 13.66 s → 6.88 s cold.** The plan's "single highest-leverage idea, and
it is untested" — caching generated textures the way the terrain field was
already cached — worked, and was held to byte-equality against `?nobake=1`
rather than to a plausible screenshot. Two of the plan's premises were wrong on
inspection: `Dungeons` was always lazy, and `Props.landmarks` was not the
landmarks at all but `PropMaterials` memoisation landing on the first caller.
`compileAsync` was closed as a **measured negative**: 3% slower, because it
resolves 134 programs against `compile`'s 112. What would move it is fewer
programs.

**Wave 1 closed.** A perf ruler that measures its own noise floor and voids the
run rather than printing a number it cannot stand behind — it promptly voided
`perf` at a floor IQR of 27% of a frame, which is the instrument working.
`seatHeightAt` at 0.000 m residual from 60 m to 3.4 km. Ablation dials, and the
rule *ablate before re-tinting* written into `BRIEF.md`.

**Nothing in the game was pressable.** `CombatSystem` bound `KeyE` to
`warpToPoint()`, and Combat runs as system 11 while Interaction runs as 21 — so
every press warped Noctis twelve metres before `Interactables.update` ran. Every
shop, hunt board, caravan, fuel pump, Regalia and NPC advertised a prompt none
of them could honour. Separately, all three RPG coordinate tables were hand-typed
against a 3 km world that later grew to 8 km, so camping answered `no-haven`
everywhere and **no `reach` objective in the game could ever complete**.

## The grade

The most valuable thing built tonight is probably the least visible: a blind A/B
judge with sealed keys, and 659 lines of *pixel-sampled* FFXV reference with the
53 plates it was drawn from.

The game scores **3/10**: six identified, zero fooled. The 4.5/10 everyone had
been flying on was stale and unfalsifiable.

And the instrument immediately paid for itself. An attempt to close the measured
`R−B +15.6` warmth gap in the colour grade moved the number by **1.4 levels** and
was reverted as a measured negative — because the grade was not the cause. Our
bright quartile is a near-neutral grey cloud blanket where FFXV's is blue sky.
Two of the judge's five ranked defects collapse into one cause in `Clouds.ts`.

## What the harness was still lying about

Twice tonight `npm run check` reported that the terrain had regressed. Both
times `heightcheck` and `driftcheck` passed standalone at 0.000 m. They were
never running: `serve()` resolved on a log line with a fifteen-second timer that
resolved **successfully** if the line never came, and a cold bake holds vite for
41 seconds before it listens. Two lanes each spent an investigation on it.

It polls the socket now, the aux port is scanned rather than assumed, and a
gate that measured nothing renders as **VOID** rather than FAIL — because a void
run that reads as a regression sends the next person after a number nobody took.

## The shape of the night

Almost every hour produced the same shape: a confident sentence in a document,
an instrument that disagreed with it, and a cause somewhere else entirely. The
plans were good and their diagnoses were frequently wrong — which is an argument
for writing plans *and* for never trusting one past the first measurement.

# Part two — the AAA half

The plans closed around the middle of the night. What follows is the second
half, which was a different kind of work: no plan to execute, one instrument to
trust, and a judge that would not move.

## Six systems that were declared and never ran

This is the single most repeated finding of the session, and no gate could see
any of it:

| system | state |
|---|---|
| `Animator.rest()` | dead, named in the rescue ledger |
| the subsurface skin model | a **measured no-op** — 0.150/255 against a 1.5 floor |
| `BossFight.resolveStrike` | never executed, in play or in the harness, until tonight |
| tree impostors, canopy cards, far rocks | all flagged **not to cast**, while 1,239 of them filled the frame |
| three's alpha-to-coverage ramp | one-sided, so half of every leaf boundary stayed binary |
| `gully` | had **never displaced a vertex, anywhere** |

Every one was documented, referenced in handoffs, and doing nothing. "It exists"
and "it runs" turned out to be entirely different claims, and this repo has no
gate that distinguishes them. That is worth building.

## Five instruments that were measuring themselves

- a **stale texel cache** with no symptom, silently costing two seconds of boot
- `check.mts` reporting a **terrain regression** when the gate never ran at all
- every capture tool silently **photographing another worktree's code**
- `bootprof` with no contention guard — 6.88 s quiet, 17.05 s loaded, same commit
- **the perf ruler**, which rendered 20 frames in one synchronous task and
  throttled itself 5×. It reported 63 fps for a game running at 190. Correlation
  with the truth: **0.107**, and the ranking *inverted* — the shot it called
  second-worst was one of the fastest.

The last one had already been written into `STATUS.md` as fact, and reported to
the human as certified, before it was caught. The rule that came out of it is
now standing practice: **before trusting a number, make the instrument report on
a case whose answer you already know.**

## The judge, and its control

`compare.mts` had a `--selftest` that checked the *shuffle* and had never checked
the *judge*. Six rounds of "6 identified, 0 fooled" while four lanes each fixed
the defect the previous round named is either a real categorical gap or a
saturated instrument, and nothing could tell those apart.

`--control` now pairs reference plates against each other. Run properly —
landscape only, no characters or HUD to let a judge identify the *game* instead
of the *render*, shuffled and interleaved with real pairs — it came back **4/4
correct on real, 5/5 declined on control, zero false positives**. The judge
discriminates. The gap is real.

Its own unprompted answer for what gives us away, twice: **authoring**. *"The
same few instances repeated."* *"One smooth noise-displaced surface."* Not a
list of rendering defects — the absence of someone having chosen.

## What that bought

Chasing it produced the session's best fixes, and almost all were one wrong
constant rather than a missing feature:

- **every trunk in the world was one colour** — the wood mesh had no per-instance
  colour buffer at all
- trees didn't lean, because the tilt was the same number in both Euler
  components, so the whole world leaned along one diagonal
- crown sway used one value on both axes, making **every crown a disc in plan** —
  which is why per-instance yaw had never changed a silhouette
- the scatter was a stratified grid: *more uniform than random*
- **`muscle` was a size knob wearing a build knob's name** — every radius carried
  the same coefficient, so the party's proportions varied by 4-6%
- the `vista_noon` "chrome-black sphere" that three lanes rebuilt as geometry was
  **`metalness: 0.8`** on the whole outpost kit
- the tree geometry ring was capped at 130 trees against 2,152 impostors, in a
  currency this renderer does not spend — raising it to 1,200 cost **+51%
  triangles, identical draw count, +0.0 ms**

## Where it ended

The judge still identifies us. It no longer says the things it used to: exposure
discipline, alpha speckle, ground scatter, contact AO, "one shared body mesh
reskinned across the party" — all gone from its comments, each after the lane
that owned it. What it says now is hair, eyes, terrain silhouette, and a
kilometre of empty Leide plain where shipped FFXV never frames one.

The gap was never one defect deep, and the honest thing this session can claim is
not that the game is AAA. It is that for the first time the distance to AAA is
**measured, attributed, and shrinking in a direction the instrument agrees with.**
